## What this adds

**Talking Heads → Brian Eno → Chris Spedding → Mickey Jupp → Nick Lowe / Dave Edmunds** is a shorter path back into the established cohort than the Bowie route. Spedding recalls playing on Eno's *Here Come the Warm Jets*. Our existing *Juppanese* evidence places Spedding on side B and Lowe/Edmunds on side A. This connects careers and album projects; it does not put those musicians together in the same Jupp session. Sources: [Spedding's firsthand session account](https://www.guitarworld.com/artists/guitarists/chris-spedding-favorite-sessions), [Jupp discography and sleeve transcription](https://www.mickeyjupp.se/lp.htm).

**John Cale supplies another bridge:** *Slow Dazzle* credits both Spedding (guitar) and Eno (keyboards/synthesizer), while *Wrong Way Up* is an Eno/Cale collaboration. The former remains album participation, without invented track assignments or keyboard models; the latter establishes the jointly billed artists rather than their individual instruments. Sources: [AllMusic credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/slow-dazzle-mw0000463566), [Eno's official discography](https://www.brian-eno.net/).

**Talking Heads → Adrian Belew → David Bowie → Nile Rodgers / Stevie Ray Vaughan** remains a separate, already-saved path. Belew's *Lodger* work and Rodgers/Vaughan's *Let's Dance* work are different projects. This step adds exact original-recording participation on “D.J.,” “Red Sails” and “Boys Keep Swinging,” plus Belew's acknowledged admiration for Bowie's combination of experimental art and pop. Sources: [Belew interview](https://www.innerviews.org/inner/adrian-belew), [Belew's Bowie recollections](https://www.loudersound.com/features/adrian-belew-david-bowie-lodger). Earlier links are documented in [Talking Heads discovery](2026-10-18-talking-heads-discovery.md).

## Recording methods worth comparing

David Byrne describes building *My Life in the Bush of Ghosts* from repeating improvised parts over a beat and carrying that approach into *Remain in Light*. The two albums now point to the same typed technique. This is a documented method connection, not merely a genre resemblance. [Library of Congress interview, pages 1–2](https://lcweb2.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/DavidByrneInterview.pdf).

For *Lodger*, Belew describes being denied advance song/key preparation, playing a few passes, and having selected moments assembled into a composite guitar part. He heard the music while playing; “unprepared” does not mean playing without audio. This is a different process from layering repeating parts, although both make spontaneous performances into arranged material. That comparison is analysis, not evidence that the methods or equipment were identical. [Innerviews](https://www.innerviews.org/inner/adrian-belew).

Spedding remembers a 16-track studio for his *Warm Jets* work. That saves a useful production constraint, without inventing a recorder, console, room identity or uniform setup for every album session. [Guitar World interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/artists/guitarists/chris-spedding-favorite-sessions).

Belew's memory of occasional Oblique Strategies use does not securely identify an album. We retained it as **artist context at 62/100**, with the uncertainty visible. It is not a *Lodger* or *Remain in Light* track credit. [Louder interview](https://www.loudersound.com/features/adrian-belew-david-bowie-lodger).

No new guitar or amplifier model was established for *Lodger*. The previously saved, recording-specific Great Curve Stratocaster/JC-120/Electric Mistress evidence and tentative Big Muff recollection remain available; those items were not copied onto Bowie's recordings. These connections suggest listening comparisons, not a proven explanation of Nick's preferences.
