## Useful connections

**Weller is part of the sound of both OCS and Oasis.** On *Moseley Shoals*, the liner notes allocate organ on “The Riverboat Song,” outro lead guitar on “The Circle,” and piano/harmony on “One for the Road.” Oasis's original “Champagne Supernova” has Weller guest lead guitar and backing vocals. These are saved as separate recording claims. [Daniel Rachel's liner notes](https://danielrachel.com/writings/ocean-colour-scene-moseley-shoals/), [Morning Glory credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/ed286309-6e64-48e3-835b-61aaf86cdb86).

**The exchanges continue in Weller's own work.** On “Echoes Round the Sun,” Cradock has a co-production credit and Noel Gallagher a performance credit. Qobuz's literal `BassVocalist` label is ambiguous; this batch does not expand it into bass guitar plus vocals. This supplements the earlier verified Gallagher acoustic-guitar contribution to “I Walk on Gilded Splinters.” [Qobuz track 14](https://www.qobuz.com/nz-en/album/22-dreams-paul-weller/0060251775709).

**The Jam's repertoire connects Liam and Cradock directly.** Their “Carnation” cover appears on *Fire and Skill*. Weller and Cradock separately recorded the hidden “No One in the World”; Noel's “To Be Someone” is another performance. The original CD stores the last two songs in one indexed track. Claims expressly apply to the second song only, rather than crediting every contributor throughout that combined recording. [Contemporary NME report](https://www.nme.com/news/music/gene-16-1388404), [1999 edition](https://musicbrainz.org/release/b5d8ae8d-38d0-4ea1-8aa5-fa894beeda97).

**“Come Together” is a shared session, with a specific guitar connection.** The Smokin' Mojo Filters charity recording brings Cradock, Weller, Noel and McCartney together. Cradock identifies Weller's 1966 Epiphone Casino as his session guitar. The conditional Bluesbreaker recollection was not included in this batch. Native MusicBrainz recording metadata separately connects producer Brendan Lynch. [Cradock interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/artists/guitarists/steve-cradock-paul-mccartney-come-together-session), [recording metadata](https://musicbrainz.org/recording/0a42fc30-c58c-4968-ae4a-fe4f0ce1cb5f).

**A physical instrument connects the Jam era to later OCS albums.** Cradock says Weller gave him a Jam-era Rickenbacker, subsequently used on *One From the Modern* and *Mechanical Wonder*. The source calls it “300”; the saved factor retains an unresolved exact model rather than merging it with the previously documented Rickenbacker 330. Separate album claims reuse that same physical-guitar factor. Cradock also identifies a Les Paul/JCM800 pairing for *Moseley Shoals*. [Firsthand guitar interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/steve-cradock-ocean-colour-scene-paul-weller).

**Brendan Lynch is a production bridge worth comparing.** His *Moseley Shoals* production and “Get Away” editing/mixing sit alongside the previously saved *Stanley Road* credit. Cradock describes a cassette-recorder-like device used near drums for distorted mono texture. That remains producer context, with no invented song allocation. The album's live-band capture and retained demo performances are separate method factors; the band-together factor is shared with the existing *Studio 150* research. [Cradock on Lynch](https://louderthanwar.com/ocean-colour-scene-marchin-already-tour-shepherds-bush-empire/), [album notes](https://danielrachel.com/writings/ocean-colour-scene-moseley-shoals/).

Cradock's *The Kundalini Target* adds a studio path through Black Barn and an unspecified Weller contribution. The latter is saved at 87/100, with instrument and track unresolved. [Artist's official album page](https://www.stevecradock.com/audio/the-kundalini-target/).
