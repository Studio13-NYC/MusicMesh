# Paul Weller: the Jam, solo work and shared sounds

Completed 18 October 2026 (New York). Nick redirected the proposed Spedding branch to Paul Weller, covering the Jam and solo work. The Style Council supplies a useful bridge between those periods. Research extends beyond listening history.

## The connections that matter

**The Jam → Roger Bechirian → Costello / Nick Lowe.** Bechirian confirms engineering a session for *All Mod Cons*. Costello's own *Armed Forces* account identifies Bechirian as engineer and Lowe as producer. This is a concrete personnel path back to the starting cohort. It does not make Lowe the Jam's producer or Bechirian engineer of every Jam track. [Bechirian interview](https://www.rocktownhall.com/blogs/roger-bechirian-interview/5/), [Costello's album notes](https://elviscostello.com/liner_notes/armed-forces/).

**The Style Council → Weller's solo musicians.** Provider membership connects Weller and Mick Talbot through the Style Council. On *Stanley Road*, Talbot plays Fender Rhodes on “Broken Stones” and pipe/Hammond organ on “Wings of Speed.” Steve White plays drums on “The Changingman.” Winwood plays piano, Hammond and Wurlitzer on “Woodcutter's Son,” but piano on “Pink on White Walls.” Gallagher supplies acoustic guitar on “I Walk on Gilded Splinters”; Cradock plays guitar/backing vocals on “The Changingman” and guitar on “Porcelain Gods.” The album is co-produced by Brendan Lynch and Weller. These distinct parts offer much more useful comparisons than a general guest list. [Transcribed original sleeve credits](https://albumlinernotes.com/Stanley_Road.html).

**Steve Cradock connects Weller to Ocean Colour Scene and a later production role.** Native membership establishes the Ocean Colour Scene association. His specific *Stanley Road* performance credit is separate from producing Weller's 2025 *Find El Dorado*. The latter is an explicit official album credit, not inferred from his longstanding band role. [Weller's official album listing](https://shop.paulweller.com/products/find-el-dorado-exclusive-lp).

**Weller → Robert Wyatt → Brian Eno.** The contemporaneous *Wire* interview/article identifies Weller's contributions to “Free Will and Testament” and “Blues in Bob Minor” on *Shleep*. It describes guitar and slide on the former. Wyatt describes Eno reworking “Heaps of Sheeps” and singing with him. This links artist networks through the same album while preserving different tracks; it does not establish Weller and Eno performing together. [Archived interview and transcript](https://www.disco-robertwyatt.com/images/Robert/interviews/Wire_september_1997/index.htm).

**Weller → “Thinking of You” → Nile Rodgers / Bernard Edwards** is a repertoire connection. The saved recording performs the Work whose writers are Rodgers and Edwards. Weller also names them in his *Studio 150* interview. That establishes a composition path, not Rodgers producing or playing on Weller's cover. Weller describes the album's band playing together in the studio. [Firsthand interview](https://elpais.com/diario/2004/09/24/espectaculos/1095976802_850215.html), [composition identity and writers](https://musicbrainz.org/work/74785e1d-f31a-34bb-94d8-e1771990a262).

## Equipment and methods

“Going Underground” now has separate, recording-specific factors for a Rickenbacker 330, Vox AC30 and roadie-built Les Paul Junior-style guitar. Weller assigns the Rickenbacker to rhythm parts and the custom instrument to many harmony leads; he describes amplifier overdrive. The arrangement developed through band jamming. These are original studio-recording claims, not defaults for later live versions.

The same article attributes “The Eton Rifles” guitar tone to corrugated iron placed around the amplifier. This detail is the writer's account within a participant interview, saved at **85/100**. Weller only suggests that the treatment might also have been used on “Going Underground”; that extension was not asserted. [Weller / MusicRadar interview](https://www.musicradar.com/news/the-jam-paul-weller-going-underground-interview).

For *Sonik Kicks*, Weller describes replacing some drum-machine loops with live drums within a Pro Tools workflow and using a Danelectro on many tracks. These are album-level claims; the guitar model and track allocation remain unspecified. [Guitar World interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/interview-paul-weller-discusses-danelectros-rickenbackers-and-his-new-album).

The later *Fat Pop* workflow combines remote exchange of click-track drafts with a return to recording together at Black Barn. This remains dated artist context rather than an invented credit for every song. [Weller interview](https://www.xsnoize.com/interview-paul-weller-on-fat-pop-volume-1-my-only-ambition-is-to-have-more-of-what-im-having-now-life-music-family-children-and-all-that/).

The taste-research hypothesis is now specific: compare the recurring keyboard colors, rhythmic ensemble interaction and individual collaborators across recordings Nick likes. This is a hypothesis to test against listening preferences, not a finding that any factor already explains those preferences.
