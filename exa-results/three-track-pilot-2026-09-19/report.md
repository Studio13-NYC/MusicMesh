# MusicMesh — three-track research pilot

Research date: 19 September 2026
Status: documentary first-pass snapshot. A later [captured-audio follow-up](audio-analysis.md) now contains computational measurements and updated identity evidence; firsthand listening remains unavailable in this session. This is a research artifact, not a change to the MusicMesh application or database.

## What this test establishes

The useful unit is the **particular recording and its contributors**, not the headline artist or a broad genre. These three selections produce genuinely different investigations:

| Selection | Presentation | What the evidence opens up |
|---|---|---|
| The Heavy Heavy — Miles and Miles (Single Version) | Band | A home-recording partnership, deliberate track-count limits, earlier projects and documented cover-song routes |
| Maggie Rogers — In the Living Room | Female solo artist | A co-writing/production partnership and a guitarist who opens a second recording community |
| Childish Gambino — Running Around, featuring Fousheé | Male solo artist with collaborators | A distributed production team, three credited guitarists, a drummer and a second vocalist/producer beyond the advertised feature |

These categories are sampling choices, not explanations of how the music sounds. No common producer, recording room, console or instrument has been established across all three. No recommendation is yet supported by firsthand listening or a new preference judgment from Nick.

## 1. The Heavy Heavy — Miles and Miles (Single Version)

### Recording identity and credits

ATO explicitly lists the single version separately and describes the June 2022 release as including a single edit. This matters: the Bandcamp and expanded-edition Qobuz entries examined list the longer 4:03 recording. Chartmetric lists the single version at 3:40; the official video search result reports 3:23. These are **different catalog objects, not interchangeable audio**. The pilot has not matched the playlist/Roon item to an ISRC or audio file. [ATO release](https://atorecords.com/releases/the-heavy-heavy-life-and-life-only/), [ATO announcement](https://atorecords.com/featured/the-heavy-heavy-releases-ep-and-new-miles-and-miles-music-video/), [single-version metadata](https://app.chartmetric.com/track/83396061).

Qobuz credits Will Turner as composer, producer and mixing engineer on the 4:03 version. That is related-recording evidence; it does not independently verify the editor or mastering chain of the single. Georgie Fuller and Turner describe the EP as home-recorded in their London flat. Neither the full track-by-track instrument roster nor the single's mastering credit was established. [Qobuz, track 4](https://www.qobuz.com/ie-en/album/life-and-life-only-the-heavy-heavy/op7en8azxjkdc), [MAGNET interview](https://magnetmagazine.com/2022/10/06/a-conversation-with-the-heavy-heavy/).

### Production, origins and influences

Turner describes combining old and new equipment while restricting the process to eight tracks. This supports a production-method claim, **not a named eight-track tape machine**. The group later expanded to five members; the touring formation should not be assigned wholesale to the EP sessions. Turner identifies Malvern as his upbringing, Fuller the area near Brighton, and Turner names his earlier psych/surf/jam group, The White Feather Collective. His song-specific account describes imagined American travel as an impetus for Miles and Miles. These geographic roles differ from the London recording location. [MAGNET](https://magnetmagazine.com/2022/10/06/a-conversation-with-the-heavy-heavy/), [artist Q&A](https://www.theselfportraitgospel.com/interviews/the-heavy-heavy-interview).

Their expanded release supplies stronger exploration paths than an unqualified “sounds like”: recorded covers of Father John Misty's **Real Love Baby**, Jonathan Wilson's **Desert Raven**, and CSN's **Guinnevere**. These establish repertoire relationships, not collaboration with the original artists. [ATO expanded edition](https://atorecords.com/releases/the-heavy-heavy-life-and-life-only-expanded-edition/).

### Technical detail found — but for a different record

In an interview about **One of a Kind**, Turner names a Fender Princeton, an early-1970s Marshall, a 1981 Les Paul Standard for the solos, and a 1960s Danelectro spring-reverb unit. He describes clean amps feeding tape hard and says he used no pedals in those studio sessions. This is worthwhile later-album evidence, **not the verified Miles and Miles rig**; the Marshall and reverb model numbers, settings, microphone chain and console remain unspecified. [Guitar World interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/artists/bands/the-heavy-heavy-one-of-a-kind).

**Next useful work:** match the single; identify the single's personnel/mastering; compare its arrangement with the longer version; listen to one documented cover route. Stop generic gear searches until there is an EP-specific source.

## 2. Maggie Rogers — In the Living Room

### Recording identity and credits

This is the standalone single released 8 October 2024, written in March 2023 after the main Don't Forget Me writing sessions. The label identifies Rogers and Ian Fitchuk as its writers/producers. Rogers' own announcement identifies Konrad Snyder as engineer, Fitchuk and Sam Evian for instrumentation, Jonathan Low for mixing and Emily Lazar for mastering. Audio credits are separated from the video crew in that post. [Universal announcement](https://www.universalmusic.ca/2024/10/08/maggie-rogers-unveils-new-single-in-the-living-room-today/), [Rogers' credits](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DA3wAGpPb9G/?hl=en).

AllMusic adds a more granular, **secondary-source** breakdown: Fitchuk on bass, drums, acoustic/electric guitar and keyboards; Evian on electric guitar and engineering; Lauren Marquez and Michael Deano assisting. Keep these finer assignments attributed until checked against original liner/distributor credits. Its track row says 4:21 while the page summary says 4:24; neither is an audio measurement. [AllMusic credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/in-the-living-room-mw0004393361).

Electric Lady is documented for the **album's** early sessions. The inspected sources do not explicitly establish the room used for this March single. Likewise, Sam Evian's own studio is not automatically the location of his contribution. Maryland is established as Rogers' origin at state level, not an exact hometown in this source set. [Album-session interview](https://www.rollingstone.co.uk/music/maggie-rogers-dont-forget-me-interview-38583/), [label biography](https://www.universalmusic.ca/2024/10/08/maggie-rogers-unveils-new-single-in-the-living-room-today/).

### A useful contributor-led route

**In the Living Room → credited instrumentalist Sam Evian → his album Plunge → recording methods and collaborators.**

Evian's interview discusses his earlier band Celestial Shore under the name Sam Owens, Figure 8 Recording, and his Catskills studio. His Plunge sessions provide a documented path toward Adrianne Lenker, El Kempner and Liam Kazar. These are a contributor's other projects, not uncredited Maggie Rogers personnel. His explanation of Glyn Johns and Get Back as workflow inspirations is especially valuable: it concerns how musicians share a room and commit performances, not just a stylistic resemblance. [Guitar Player interview](https://www.guitarplayer.com/players/sam-evian-plunge), [Our Culture Q&A](https://ourculturemag.com/2024/03/25/sam-evian-plunge-interview/).

### Model-level production detail — correctly attached to Plunge

| Component | What Evian's interview identifies | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Custom guitar | Warmoth-based Strat-shaped build, Gibson-scale deep-V neck, Lollar gold-foil pickups | Not a factory model; no pickup measurements/settings |
| Effects | Origin Effects Cali76, Wampler Tumnus, Chase Bliss Warped Vinyl | Pedal ordering and exact settings not established |
| Amplifier | Magnatone Twilighter | No control positions |
| Capture | Beyerdynamic M 160 into a Neve preamp | Neve model not given |
| Recording | Tascam 48 eight-track, then transfer to an MCI 16-track two-inch machine for completion | MCI model not given |
| Session method | Early takes, musicians together, no click/headphones, room bleed | Album-level account, not a universal practice |

These details are a **successful research branch**, not Maggie's signal chain. The interview also identifies a 1962 Epiphone Sorrento; no evidence assigns either guitar to her single. A second interview describes a 1974 console but supplies no make/model. [Guitar Player](https://www.guitarplayer.com/players/sam-evian-plunge), [Our Culture](https://ourculturemag.com/2024/03/25/sam-evian-plunge-interview/).

**Next useful work:** establish where and how Evian's contribution was recorded; listen for the guitar part before seeking its gear; compare a specific Plunge track such as Why Does It Take So Long? only as a candidate, not a predicted preference.

[Track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/ca-en/album/bando-stone-and-the-new-world-childish-gambino/buzikcn8shlqb), [artist release entry](https://audiomack.com/childish-gambino/song/running-around-0632129).

### A rhythm-section exploration route

Aaron Steele's own portfolio includes work with Hayley Williams, Portugal. The Man, Leon Bridges, José James and others, with **Simmer** and **Sudden Desire** among its demo selections. This provides named candidates for a contributor-led listening comparison; a demo placement alone does not certify every instrumental credit. An earlier interview page identifies his New York upbringing and discusses Bill Withers and At the Drive-In in his musical background. Neither proves that Running Around consciously references those artists. [Steele's portfolio](https://network.landr.com/users/aaron-gilbert-steele), [The Trap Set](https://www.thetrapset.net/148-aaron-steele-chrome-sparks-portugal-the-man-alice-smith/).

### Mixing evidence and its boundary

Stuart White's original Audiopunks interview discusses this album, preserving production intent, stereo placement and revisions. Its concrete examples concern **Hearts Were Meant to Fly** and **We Are God**. The retrieved transcript does not explicitly identify a Running Around breakdown. It supports researching White's methods, **not assigning those effects to this track**. Names in the automatic transcript contain errors; no uncertain device name has been promoted into a model-level claim. [Original interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0KhkOzu-bNc).

No track-specific studio/room, console, guitar models, drum sizes/tuning, mic placement, effects chain or settings were established. A 2019 Mackin equipment account surfaced but predates this release and was not used as its rig. Glover's hometown and former-group history were not independently researched in this bounded pass.

**Next useful work:** resolve Mackin/Trevena/Steele session accounts and identify which guitar parts each played; obtain Running Around-specific mix evidence; check original credits for one Steele comparison track.

## What connects — and what does not

1. **Documented personnel paths:** Rogers → Evian → his recording circle; Gambino → Steele → his other projects. These survive a genre change without pretending every connected track will fit Nick's taste.
2. **Documented repertoire paths:** The Heavy Heavy's covers offer precise musical relationships. Covering, influencing, collaborating and merely resembling must remain different relationships.
3. **Analytical inference, not a historical link:** Turner's EP-era eight-track constraint and Evian's Plunge method suggest a promising comparison around commitment and limited tracks. No shared machine, session or direct influence between them was found.
4. **No supported sonic conclusion yet:** Shared instrumentation, different team sizes, label affiliation and origin do not establish shared sound or preference.

The most useful output is not a dense graph joining everything. It is a short path whose evidence and scope can be inspected, with an honest point at which the path becomes a hypothesis.

## Firsthand audible analysis — pending, not replaced

At the time of this documentary first pass, no audio had been listened to or measured. The subsequent [captured-audio follow-up](audio-analysis.md) measures levels, spectral balance, stereo relationships and rhythmic periodicities. Published descriptions remain attributed accounts; none of these measurements is presented as firsthand listening. Key, chord progression, groove/microtiming, harmonic rhythm, vocal layering, saturation and reverb remain unverified.

The listening pass should use the exact matched recording and record:

| Layer | Capture |
|---|---|
| Perception | Timestamped observation: e.g., a second vocal enters, or a guitar occupies the right channel |
| Musical interpretation | Arrangement, groove, harmony and production hypotheses, explicitly labeled inference |
| Alternatives | For example, perceived doubling could be two performances, delay, chorus or a combination |
| Measurement | Method and result, where useful; measurement is not proof of the production cause |
| Documentary link | Which credit/interview supports, contradicts or fails to address the inference |
| Preference | Nick's response to this passage and context, separate from artist liking |

Do an initial listening description before displaying gear details where practical, to reduce priming. Never identify a specific guitar, pedal, console or tape machine from a finished master alone. If only an excerpt is available, constrain conclusions to that excerpt.

## What the pilot suggests for research execution

**Baseline, then follow evidence-rich branches** worked better here than either stopping at generic credits or trying to fill every possible field equally.

- Establish recording identity before detailed sonic comparisons; do not let missing identifiers prevent properly scoped documentary research.
- Separate exact-track credits, parent-session accounts, other-release facts and artist background.
- Open original interviews after search; results sometimes resolved to irrelevant site homepages.
- Track copied label biographies and shared distributor data as common provenance, not independent corroboration.
- Prefer one useful, evidenced branch per seed before expanding to dozens of connections.
- Stop a branch when the next step needs audio, liner notes, access or participant confirmation; save that need precisely.
- Let coverage differ. This pass is stronger on Heavy Heavy's formation, Maggie's contributor network and Gambino's track-specific personnel than on exact gear for any seed.

For eventual UI comparison, a **three-column investigation view** earns a trial: each track shows established facts, contextual leads, listening notes and open questions. Small relationship paths can be opened from it. A graph-first display risks hiding the scope distinctions this pilot surfaced. This is a design implication, not an implemented screen.

## Files and audit boundary

- **report.md** — this readable synthesis.
- **evidence.json** — selected claims, sources, scope, confidence rationale, exclusions and next actions; a research snapshot, not a proposed application schema.
- **search-log.json** — actual queries and URL-level discovery/fetch records, without copied full articles.

Eighteen searches requested 90 result slots across three seed investigations and their follow-up branches, deduplicated to 67 distinct URLs. These include irrelevant homepages and search-only leads: **90 is not a count of independent sources read**. Twenty-one distinct pages were fetched for closer inspection, including a partial AP extraction. The log records the distinctions. This is a bounded first pass, not exhaustive coverage or a validated recommendation engine.
